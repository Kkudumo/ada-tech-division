Virus removal: does my computer have a virus?

Most “virus warnings” that fill the screen and give a phone number are not viruses. They are web pages built to frighten you into calling. Real infections are quieter.

Get it diagnosedAsk on WhatsApp
In short

A full-screen warning that tells you to call a number is a scam web page, not a virus. Close the browser and never call the number or give anyone remote access. Real infections show up as programs you did not install, a browser that redirects, or security software that has been switched off. Run a full scan with Windows Security, remove unknown browser extensions and change your passwords from another device.

Self-checks

What you can check yourself

These are safe to try, cost nothing and do not need tools.

  1. Close a fake warning

    Press Alt + F4, or press Ctrl + Shift + Esc and end the browser in Task Manager. Do not call the number and do not click inside the page.

  2. Remove notification permissions

    In Chrome or Edge, open Settings, Privacy and security, Site settings, Notifications and remove every site you do not recognise.

  3. Remove unknown extensions

    Open the browser's Extensions page and remove anything you did not choose to install.

  4. Run a full scan

    Windows Security, Virus & threat protection, Scan options, Full scan. Then run Microsoft Defender Antivirus (offline scan), which restarts the PC and scans before Windows loads.

  5. Uninstall what you do not recognise

    Settings, Apps, Installed apps, sorted by install date. Remove programs that appeared around the time the trouble started.

  6. Change your passwords from another device

    Use a phone or a different computer. Start with email and banking, and turn on two-step verification.

  7. Install pending updates

    Updates close the holes that malware uses.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need to buy antivirus software?

Windows Security is built into Windows 10 and 11 and is enough for most people when it is switched on and Windows is kept up to date. Running two antivirus programs together slows the computer and causes conflicts.

I called the number on the warning. What now?

If you gave remote access, disconnect the computer from the internet. If you gave card details, call your bank straight away. Change your passwords from another device, then have the computer checked.

Does ADA Tech ever phone people to say their computer has a virus?

No. Nobody legitimate does. A call or a pop-up that arrives unasked and offers to fix your computer is a scam.
